Orangeville Public Library has two branches in Orangeville, Ontario. The Mill Street Library is at 1 Mill Street, at the corner of Mill Street and Broadway. The Alder Street Library is at 275 Alder Street, on the second floor of the Alder Recreation Centre. One library card works at both branches, and borrowed items can be returned to either one. The phone number for both branches is 519-941-0610. The website is orangevillelibrary.ca.

Opening hours for the Mill Street Library, 1 Mill Street, Orangeville. Monday to Friday: 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Saturday: 10 a.m. to 5 p.m. Sunday: 12 noon to 4 p.m. A book drop box is available when the branch is closed. Hours can change on holidays, so check the library website or call 519-941-0610.

Opening hours for the Alder Street Library, 275 Alder Street, Orangeville, inside the Alder Recreation Centre. Monday and Wednesday: 2 p.m. to 8 p.m. Tuesday and Thursday: 10 a.m. to 8 p.m. Friday: 10 a.m. to 6 p.m. Saturday: 9 a.m. to 4 p.m. No Sunday hours are posted for the Alder Street branch. A return drop box is in the main lobby of the recreation centre. Hours can change on holidays, so check the library website or call 519-941-0610.

What you will find at the Mill Street Library, 1 Mill Street: two quiet study spaces, two meeting rooms that can be rented, six public computer workstations, touch-screen catalogue stations, a printer with a scanner, free Wi-Fi, more than 60 magazines, a local history workstation with a microfilm reader, a light therapy lamp, and a children's area called Storyland. Programs for kids and adults are held here. Study spaces, computers, the local history workstation and the light therapy lamp can be booked with the Request a Reservation form on the library website. The Makerspace and the 3D printer are not at Mill Street; they are at the Alder Street Library.

What you will find at the Alder Street Library, 275 Alder Street, on the second floor of the Alder Recreation Centre: the library's Makerspace, including the 3D printer and the laser cutter, programs for kids, six public computer workstations, touch-screen catalogue stations, a printer with a scanner, and free Wi-Fi. There is elevator access to the second floor. Computers can be booked with the Request a Reservation form on the library website.

Parking at Orangeville Public Library. At the Mill Street Library there is street parking for up to two hours, plus parking lots at the corner of Mill and Church Streets and off Wellington and Little York Streets. Accessible parking spaces are across from the Mill Street entrance. At the Alder Street Library, in the Alder Recreation Centre, parking is free and accessible spaces are near the entrance. Orangeville Transit is free to ride until July 1, 2027.

Holiday closures at Orangeville Public Library for the rest of 2026. Both branches are closed for Thanksgiving weekend on Sunday, October 11 and Monday, October 12. Both are closed on Monday, November 16 for staff training. Both close early, at 4 p.m., on Thursday, December 31. Both are closed from December 25 to December 28, and on January 1, 2027.

How to contact Orangeville Public Library. The main phone number is 519-941-0610. It is used for accounts and renewals, general information, programs, room rentals, library tours and the homebound delivery service. Useful extensions: the administrative assistant at extension 5221 for room rentals and exam proctoring, and the public services librarian at extension 5232 for homebound delivery. The mailing address is 1 Mill Street, Orangeville, Ontario, L9W 2M2. The Contact Us page on the library website has email links.

How to get an Orangeville Public Library card. A card is free for people who live in Orangeville. Apply with the online form on the library website or in person at either branch, and bring ID that shows your current address. Children 13 and under need a parent or guardian with them. A card is good for one year and is renewed by visiting the library. A replacement for a lost card costs 2 dollars. Tell the library if your address, phone number or email changes.

Library cards for people who live outside Orangeville. Residents of Mono, Amaranth and East Garafraxa pay nothing, because their municipality pays the fee, but they sign an agreement. Businesses and property owners in Orangeville get a free card by showing a business card or a document with the business address. Anyone else can buy a yearly membership for 210 dollars per household, paid by cash, debit or cheque. Out-of-town visitors can ask for a guest pass to use a library computer.

What an Orangeville Public Library card lets you do: borrow books, movies and other items; download e-books and audiobooks; read magazines and newspapers online; use library computers; use the Makerspace; and sign in to your account to see due dates, place holds and renew items. The library emails a reminder three days before items are due, and again when a hold is ready. There is also an Orangeville Public Library app for iPhone and Android.

How long you can borrow items from Orangeville Public Library. Books and magazines: 28 days, up to 50 items. Audiobooks: 28 days, up to 10. DVDs: 7 days, up to 5. DVD sets: 28 days. Puzzles: 28 days, 2 at a time. Wi-Fi SmartSpots, Chromebooks, and park or museum passes: 7 days. Book club kits and radon detectors: 6 weeks. Most items can be renewed up to 3 times if nobody else is waiting. Passes, Chromebooks and book club kits cannot be renewed.

Late fees at Orangeville Public Library. There are no overdue fines on books, audiobooks, magazines, DVDs and puzzles. Late fees of 1 dollar 50 cents per day apply to the Passes and Things collection, such as Wi-Fi SmartSpots, Chromebooks, park and museum passes, learning tablets and STEM kits. Book club kits are 20 cents per day for each book. Items borrowed from other libraries are 2 dollars per day. An item is treated as lost when it is 6 weeks overdue, and you are charged its replacement cost plus a 5 dollar fee. Borrowing is suspended when you owe 50 dollars or more. Payment is by cash, debit or cheque.

Renewing and returning items at Orangeville Public Library. Renew online through your account, by email, by phone at 519-941-0610, or in person. Items can be returned to either branch. Return drop boxes are open 24 hours a day at the Mill Street Library and in the lobby of the Alder Recreation Centre. Wi-Fi SmartSpots and Chromebooks must be handed back to staff inside the library. If you are going away, ask at checkout about a longer vacation loan.

Public computers at Orangeville Public Library. Both branches have six computer workstations with Microsoft Word and Excel. Sign in with your library card number and four-digit PIN. Out-of-town visitors can ask for a guest pass. You can use a computer for up to two hours a day. Computers are first come, first served, or you can reserve one up to a week ahead with the Request a Reservation form on the library website. No food or drink near the computers. Each branch also has an accessible computer workstation.

Wi-Fi at Orangeville Public Library. Free Wi-Fi is available at both the Mill Street and Alder Street branches. You can also borrow a Wi-Fi SmartSpot, a portable hotspot that works anywhere in Ontario with Rogers network coverage. SmartSpots are for full members aged 14 and older, one per household, for 7 days. Each one has 1 gigabyte of data per day. The late fee is 1 dollar 50 cents per day, and a lost SmartSpot costs 200 dollars to replace.

Printing and photocopying prices at Orangeville Public Library, at both branches. Black and white is 20 cents per side. Colour is 50 cents per side. Paper is letter size. Scanning costs 50 cents for up to 25 pages. Sending a fax costs 3 dollars for the first page and 1 dollar for each extra page, within Canada only. Printouts are paid for and collected at the service desk.

How to print from your own phone, tablet or laptop at Orangeville Public Library. The library uses a service called Princh. At the library, scan the QR code on the poster beside the printer. From anywhere else, go to print.princh.com and enter the printer code: 111071 for the Mill Street Library or 111070 for the Alder Street Library. Upload your document, choose black and white or colour, and enter your email address. Then go to the service desk within 24 hours to pay and collect your pages. Nothing is printed until you pick it up.

Chromebooks for students at Orangeville Public Library. Upper Grand District School Board students can borrow a Chromebook from either branch for one week. They need their school board login and a library card. Students aged 14 or older can borrow one themselves; for younger students a parent or guardian borrows it. Chromebooks cannot be renewed and must be returned inside the library.

Technology help at Orangeville Public Library. Staff offer tech help as drop-in sessions at either branch or as one-on-one appointments, which you request with a form on the library website. You can also learn on your own through the library: LinkedIn Learning has more than 16,000 video courses, and the OPL Digital Learning Academy has short tutorial videos about the library's online services.

Meeting rooms at Orangeville Public Library are at the Mill Street Library, 1 Mill Street. The meeting room holds up to 60 people in rows or 40 at tables, and has tables, chairs, a podium and a wall-mounted projector. It costs 40 dollars per hour plus tax, or 20 dollars per hour plus tax for non-profit groups. The boardroom holds 12 people and costs 30 dollars per hour plus tax, or 15 dollars for non-profit groups. Rooms can be booked up to 90 days ahead, during library opening hours. They are not available for private parties such as birthdays or showers. To book, call 519-941-0610 extension 5221.

Quiet study space at Orangeville Public Library. The Mill Street Library has two quiet study spaces. Book one with the Request a Reservation form on the library website, or ask staff. The same form is used to reserve a public computer, the local history workstation, or time with the light therapy lamp.

Exam proctoring at Orangeville Public Library. The library supervises exams for students of any age who are studying by distance education. It is free for library card holders and costs 40 dollars plus tax for non-residents. Book about three weeks ahead with the proctoring request form on the library website. Exams are held during regular opening hours. Exams that need software installed on a library computer cannot be accommodated. Rescheduling with less than 48 hours notice costs 25 dollars. For questions call 519-941-0610 extension 5221.

Homebound delivery from Orangeville Public Library. Orangeville residents who cannot get to the library because of age, illness or limited mobility can have library materials brought to them. Staff choose items based on what the person likes to read, and a volunteer delivers them once a month to the home or retirement residence and collects the previous items. A family member or friend can also pick up on someone's behalf. To sign up, call 519-941-0610 extension 5232 or use the application form on the library website.

Library tours and outreach visits. Groups of any age can book a tour of either Orangeville Public Library branch. A tour lasts about 45 minutes to an hour and can include a story and activity for children, help finding information, or a demonstration. Library staff can also come to your event or location with a speaker, a display, a storytime or an activity. Book ahead with the tour request form on the library website, or call 519-941-0610.

Garbage bag tags and transit. County of Dufferin garbage bag tags are sold at both Orangeville Public Library branches. The library accepts cash and debit for them. Orangeville Transit is free to ride until July 1, 2027, so no bus pass is needed.

Accessibility at Orangeville Public Library. The Mill Street Library has accessible public washrooms, an elevator, an accessible computer workstation, and accessible parking across from the Mill Street entrance. The Alder Street Library has elevator access to the second floor, an accessible computer workstation, and accessible parking near the entrance. Guide dogs and other service animals are welcome. The library lends large print books, audiobooks on CD, and DAISY talking books and players. E-books let you make the text larger. To comment on accessible services, call 519-941-0610.

Books for people who cannot read regular print. Orangeville Public Library is a member of CELA, the Centre for Equitable Library Access. CELA offers more than 700,000 books, magazines and newspapers in accessible formats such as audio, e-text and braille, by download or by mail. It is for people with a learning, physical or visual disability that makes conventional print hard to read. Sign up through CELA with your library card, or ask library staff to help.

Books at Orangeville Public Library. The library has more than 50,000 print books for adults, teens and children. With a library card you can also borrow e-books and audiobooks with the Libby app, which has thousands of popular titles in more than 50 languages, and with hoopla, which has more than a million e-books, audiobooks, comics, albums, movies and TV shows. If you cannot find a title in the catalogue, use the Request an Item form on the library website and a librarian will try to get it.

Online services available with an Orangeville Public Library card: Libby for e-books, audiobooks and magazines; hoopla for books, music, movies and TV; Kanopy for more than 30,000 films and documentaries; PressReader for newspapers and magazines from over 100 countries; LinkedIn Learning for video courses; TumbleBookLibrary for children's animated picture books in English, French and Spanish; Novelist Plus for reading suggestions; CBC Corner for Canadian news and shows; Newspapers.com and the Toronto Star Archive for old newspapers; and G1 practice driving tests. Ancestry Library Edition can only be used inside the library.

Movies and TV at Orangeville Public Library. You can borrow DVDs of films and TV series for all ages, including documentaries, classics and world cinema. Single DVDs go out for 7 days and DVD sets for 28 days, up to 5 at a time, with no late fines. To stream instead, use Kanopy or hoopla with your library card.

Magazines and newspapers at Orangeville Public Library. The Mill Street Library has more than 60 print magazines to read or borrow. Online, PressReader has newspapers and magazines from more than 100 countries in over 60 languages, and Libby has digital magazines. For old newspapers, Newspapers.com has more than 4,000 historical papers, including early Orangeville papers, and the Toronto Star Archive goes back to 1894.

What Orangeville Public Library has for children. Picture books, chapter books, comics and graphic novels; Wonderbooks, which are print books with a built-in audio player; read-along book and CD kits; Launchpad learning tablets loaded with reading apps and games; STEM kits; puzzles; and French books for kindergarten to grade 6. Online there is TumbleBookLibrary and a kids' section in Libby. The Mill Street Library has a children's area called Storyland. Free storytimes run at both branches.

Learning a language at Orangeville Public Library. The library has books and audiobooks for learning French, Spanish, Arabic and other languages, books for learning English as a second language, and practice books for the IELTS, TOEFL and CELPIP tests. The Mauril app for learning English or French is available through CBC Corner. Libby, hoopla and PressReader offer reading and listening in dozens of languages. There are also French children's books at both branches, and Arabic picture books.

Local history and genealogy at Orangeville Public Library. The local history collection is at the Mill Street Library, with a dedicated computer and a microfilm reader that you can reserve. Microfilm includes the Orangeville Sun from 1868 to 1899, the Orangeville Banner from 1907 to 2016, and town tax assessment records from 1865 to 1900. Ancestry Library Edition is available for family history research, but only inside the library. Newspapers.com can be used at the library or from home. Staff can help you get started.

Free passes you can borrow from Orangeville Public Library. An Ontario Parks pass gives day use for one vehicle at more than 100 provincial parks, for one week. A Conservation Family Pass covers up to six people in one vehicle at Credit Valley and Toronto and Region conservation parks, for 7 days. A Museum of Dufferin pass admits two adults and up to four children, for 7 days. Theatre Orangeville passes give free seats at the current show; ask staff at the Mill Street Library, one set per household each season. Park, conservation and museum passes cannot be renewed, and late fees apply.

Unusual things you can borrow from Orangeville Public Library: a light therapy lamp for the winter blues, which can also be used at the Mill Street Library for up to 30 minutes a day; a birding backpack with binoculars and a bird guide; jigsaw puzzles from 300 to 1,000 pieces; a power meter that shows how much electricity an appliance uses; a digital radon detector, available from October to April for 6 weeks; a hand-washing teaching kit; Wi-Fi SmartSpots; learning tablets; and STEM kits.

Book club kits at Orangeville Public Library. A kit has several copies of one popular book plus a discussion guide with questions. Kits are loaned for 6 weeks and can be reserved up to a year ahead by contacting the library. Book clubs can register using a form on the library website.

History of Orangeville Public Library. It began around 1878 as the Orangeville Mechanics Institute, a members-only club, and opened to the public in 1895. With help from the Andrew Carnegie Foundation, a new library was built at the corner of Mill Street and Broadway and opened in April 1908. In the late 1980s it was joined to the old Bank of Commerce building. The building was given heritage designation in 2002. The Alder Street branch opened in 2004 on the second floor of the Alder recreation complex. Renovations between 2013 and 2024 added an elevator, more space, an atrium and the Storyland children's area.

The Friends of the Orangeville Public Library is a volunteer group that supports the library through advocacy, fundraising events, seasonal decorating and help with programs. Membership costs 5 dollars. The Friends meet four to six times a year on Tuesdays at 10:15 a.m. at the Mill Street Library, with no meetings in summer. To join, come to a meeting. Dates are posted on the Friends bulletin board in the library.
