The Makerspace at Orangeville Public Library is a creative workshop where people can try new technology, learn practical skills and make things. It is at the Alder Street Library, 275 Alder Street, on the second floor of the Alder Recreation Centre. Using the equipment is free. You pay only for materials supplied by the library, such as 3D printer filament, vinyl or wood sheets. Equipment includes a 3D printer, a laser cutter, a Cricut cutting machine, sewing, serger and embroidery machines, a button maker, scanners for photos, slides and film, tools to convert VHS and cassette tapes, a 360 degree camera and coding robots.

Who can use the Makerspace at Orangeville Public Library. You need an Orangeville Public Library card in good standing, valid photo ID such as a driver's licence or student card, and a signed Makerspace waiver. You are also asked to read the Makerspace Guidelines first. Anyone under 18 needs the waiver signed by a parent or legal guardian. Children under 14 must be with a parent or legal guardian the whole time, and that adult is treated as the main user. The exception is organised programs such as class visits. The waiver and the guidelines are on the Makerspace page of the library website.

Makerspace membership options at Orangeville Public Library. A regular library card in good standing gives access to the Makerspace. The library also sells a Makerspace Only membership for 75 dollars per household. It covers the Makerspace and its tools only, not other library services, and it does not include the cost of materials. A day pass is available for 5 dollars per household. Payment is by cash, debit or cheque.

When the Makerspace is open. The Makerspace is at the Alder Street Library and has open lab hours that change from week to week. This week's open lab hours are posted on the Makerspace page of the library website, or you can ask at the Alder Street service desk or call 519-941-0610. Booking a machine in advance and one-on-one tutorials with staff are described as coming soon. For now, the way to learn a tool is to attend a Makerspace program, listed in the library's events calendar. The space has a capacity limit.

Makerspace rules at Orangeville Public Library. No food or drinks. Equipment stays in the Makerspace and cannot be taken away. Save your work on your own USB drive or memory card, and delete your files from library computers when you finish. Makerspace computers are for creative digital projects, not for email or general browsing. You may bring your own materials such as vinyl, paper or thread, but staff must approve them before use. The space is for lawful, safe projects only. The person who reserved the equipment is responsible for any damage or misuse.

Help from staff in the Makerspace at Orangeville Public Library. Library staff give limited assistance and are not experts on every machine. If you need more help, ask for one-on-one assistance. The main way to learn a machine is to attend a Makerspace program. Each equipment page on the library website also links to manuals and video tutorials.

The 3D printer at Orangeville Public Library is a Bambu Lab P2S. It is a fast, enclosed 3D printer that can make detailed objects in more than one colour. It is in the Makerspace at the Alder Street Library, 275 Alder Street, on the second floor of the Alder Recreation Centre. Using the printer is free. You pay only for the plastic filament your print uses.

How much 3D printing costs at Orangeville Public Library. Using the Bambu Lab P2S 3D printer is free. PLA filament costs 5 cents per gram used. For example, a small print that uses 20 grams of filament costs 1 dollar, and a print that uses 100 grams costs 5 dollars. At the moment only filament bought by the library can be used in this printer, so you cannot bring your own filament.

What you can print on the library's 3D printer and where designs come from. You can use a ready-made design from an online model library such as MakerWorld, or design your own object with a beginner tool such as Tinkercad or with more advanced 3D design software. The library's web page for the printer links to tutorials for Bambu Studio, the program used to prepare a model for printing, and to the Bambu Lab P2S user guide.

Rules for 3D printing at Orangeville Public Library. The 3D printer may not be used to copy anything protected by copyright, patent or trademark. Prints must be legal, safe and appropriate for a public library; nothing dangerous, harmful or obscene may be made. The usual Makerspace rules apply: you need a library card in good standing, photo ID and a signed waiver, anyone under 18 needs a parent or guardian to sign the waiver, and children under 14 must be with a parent or guardian.

How to get started with 3D printing at Orangeville Public Library. The library website does not describe a step-by-step process for starting a print. Booking time on a machine and one-on-one tutorials are coming soon. Until then, the library teaches people to use the 3D printer through Makerspace programs, which are listed in the events calendar on the library website. For anything else, ask staff at the Alder Street service desk or call 519-941-0610. Makerspace users are asked to save their work on their own USB drive or memory card.

The laser cutter in the Orangeville Public Library Makerspace is an xTool P2S, a desktop machine with a 55 watt CO2 laser. It cuts shapes and engraves artwork, text and photos on many materials. It is used with a computer and the xTool Studio software. Using it is free; you pay for materials. The library sells walnut plywood sheets, 12 by 12 inches, for 8 dollars, acrylic sheets, 12 by 12 inches, for 15 dollars, and metal business cards for 25 cents each. You may bring your own materials, but staff must check them first. Materials with a finish are not allowed because they give off toxic fumes. Protective eyewear must be worn and you must never look directly at the laser.

The Cricut Maker 3 in the Orangeville Public Library Makerspace cuts designs sent from a computer out of thin materials such as vinyl, paper and cardstock. People use it for stickers, heat transfer designs for clothing, party decorations and greeting cards. The blade can be swapped for other blades or pens. Material prices: permanent or temporary vinyl is 3 dollars per 12 by 12 inch sheet, heat transfer vinyl is 10 dollars per 12 by 12 inch sheet, and cardstock is 25 cents. You may bring your own materials, preferably Cricut brand, but check with staff first. Designs are made in Cricut Design Space.

Sewing machines in the Orangeville Public Library Makerspace. The Janome 2030 DC-G is a computerized sewing machine with 30 built-in stitches and 3 one-step buttonholes, good for clothing, home decor and crafts. The Elna eXtend 864Air is a serger with air threading that finishes fabric edges so they do not fray, and makes seams, hems and decorative edges. Both are free to use. You bring your own thread and needles, and staff must approve them before use.

The embroidery machine in the Orangeville Public Library Makerspace is a Janome Memory Craft 550E. It adds monograms and detailed embroidery using built-in designs, or your own design loaded from a USB drive. It has several hoop sizes. The cost is 1 dollar plus 50 cents per 1,000 stitches. The library provides the needles and thread.

The button maker in the Orangeville Public Library Makerspace makes custom pin-back buttons in two round sizes, 1.5 inches and 2.25 inches. Library members aged 14 and up can use it for one hour at a time during Alder Street Library opening hours. Ask at the Alder Street service desk. The machine is free to use; you pay for parts. Small buttons are 25 cents each or 1 dollar for a pack of 5. Large buttons are 50 cents each or 2 dollars for a pack of 5.

Scanning old photos, slides and film at the Orangeville Public Library Makerspace. The Epson FastFoto FF-640 scans stacks of printed photos, including 4 by 6, 5 by 7 and 8 by 10 inch prints. The Wolverine F2D Titan turns slides, negatives and Super 8 film into digital image files saved on a USB drive or SD card. Both are free to use. Bring a USB drive or SD card to take your files home, or use cloud storage or email. USB drives and SD cards are also sold at the library service desk for about 10 to 20 dollars.

Converting VHS tapes and audio cassettes at the Orangeville Public Library Makerspace. The Elgato Video Capture turns VHS tapes into digital video files using a VHS player connected to a computer. The Reshow Cassette Capture turns cassette tapes into digital audio files using the Audacity program. Both are free to use. Bring a USB drive or use cloud storage to take your files home; video files are usually too large to email. USB drives are also sold at the library service desk for about 10 to 20 dollars.

Other Makerspace equipment at Orangeville Public Library. The Insta360 X5 camera records photos and video in a full 360 degrees, for virtual reality and augmented reality projects. Sphero BOLT is a light-up robot ball that you program from a tablet, and the library can provide the tablet. Ozobot Evo is a tiny robot that follows lines and colour codes you draw, and it can also be programmed with the Ozobot Blockly editor on a library computer. All three are free to use.

Full list of equipment in the Orangeville Public Library Makerspace at the Alder Street Library: Bambu Lab P2S 3D printer; xTool P2S laser cutter; Cricut Maker 3 cutting machine; Janome 2030 DC-G sewing machine; Elna eXtend 864Air serger; Janome Memory Craft 550E embroidery machine; button maker; Epson FastFoto FF-640 photo scanner; Wolverine F2D Titan film and slide scanner; Elgato Video Capture for VHS tapes; Reshow Cassette Capture for audio cassettes; Insta360 X5 360 degree camera; Sphero BOLT coding robot; and Ozobot Evo coding robot.
